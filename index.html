Project Detail
SAE Aero Design East 2026 · Advanced Class · Team Arrow Advanced-224
Hybrid VTOL Tricopter UAV
Overview

Hybrid tilt-rotor tricopter for SAE Aero Design East 2026 Advanced Class. Two front motors tilt 110° for VTOL; a fixed rear motor provides pitch authority during transition. The onboard Autonomous Ground Vehicle (AGV) handles payload delivery and capture, which unlocks a 14× scoring multiplier not available to manually flown aircraft. Role: Team Captain — airframe configuration, structural design, propulsion, stability, and flight test.

Contribution
  • Scoring sensitivity analysis — autonomous-first strategy projects 52 pts vs. ~30 for high-payload manual; drove configuration choice toward a lighter, structurally simpler airframe
  • Defined airframe: lifting-body fuselage (NACA 9325) + S1223 120% wing + dual-boom CFRP empennage + inverted tricycle landing gear
  • Propulsion sizing: T-Motor F80 Pro 2500 KV × 3, 6040 props, 4S LiPo — 4 min hover confirmed at 50% throttle
  • Stability analysis in XFLR5: static margin 6.37% when loaded (a measure of how strongly the aircraft returns to level flight after a disturbance); short-period damping ζ = 0.72 (well-damped — oscillations die out quickly)
  • ANSYS FEA across 4 load cases; min FoS 1.69 on all primary structure; modal analysis confirmed no resonance at cruise throttle
  • Transition flight test — validated that the aircraft only switches from hover to forward flight once it reaches a safe minimum airspeed (1.3 times the stall speed), and confirmed all failsafe protocols in Mission Planner

Engineering Decisions

Autonomous-First Configuration — Scoring analysis showed a 0.5 lb AGV payload on a lighter aircraft outscores a 1.4 lb manually dropped payload. This drove the structural target: minimise airframe weight to stay within the 3.50 lb limit while carrying the AGV. The resulting configuration is mechanically simpler than a high-payload fixed-wing — shorter spar, lighter empennage — at the cost of AGV integration complexity.

S1223 at 120% Thickness + NACA 9325 Fuselage — S1223 gives the highest Cl/Cd (2.30) at low-speed, high-lift conditions. Thickening to 120% improves structural depth for the spar without a significant drag penalty; at 130–140% the ratio drops sharply to 32, making 120% the clear optimum. The NACA 9325 fuselage section contributes secondary lift and provides internal volume for electronics, avoiding external fairings.

Inverted Tricycle Landing Gear (CFRP + PETG) — Electronics forward of CG means more nose load at landing. Two front struts (10 mm CFRP, PETG clamps) at 20° forward incline carry this bias without a heavy nose-gear spring. Rear MG90S-steered wheel gives precise positioning for AGV deployment. FEA-validated under 120 N VTOL vertical landing load — FoS 1.94.

Validation
  • CFD (ANSYS Fluent, full-body at 30 m/s): 28 N lift, 9.2 N drag with propeller downwash — confirms lift margin over 1.55 kg MTOW.
  • FEA — Static (4 load cases): Min FoS 1.69 (conventional landing, Aeroply empennage). Max deformation 2.698 mm under VTOL hover load.
  • Modal + Harmonic: No resonance at flight controller mount across 120–210 Hz. Front motor mount peak 3.18 g — within acceptable limits.
  • Stability (XFLR5): SM 6.37% loaded. Short-period ζ = 0.72. Dutch roll decays within 2 s. Spiral mode weakly unstable — autopilot-corrected.
  • Wing rib topology (Fusion 360): ~15% mass reduction per rib; spar and shear-web mounting geometry preserved.
Limitations & Next Steps
  • Spiral mode instability — dihedral or vertical tail area increase would provide design-level fix beyond autopilot correction
  • Natural frequency overlap at 25–35% and 45–55% throttle — vibration-damping motor mounts would reduce 3.18 g front mount peak
  • AGV final alignment relies on mechanical funnel; vision-based guidance (downward camera) would further reduce docking failure risk

AeroTHON 2025 · Disaster Response UAV · Team UIN2580
AeroTHON Disaster-Response Quadcopter UAV
Overview

X-frame quadcopter (ARW440S) for AeroTHON 2025. The primary design challenge was the airframe: a single-piece Nylon-12 SLS monocoque produced through a 3-phase generative design process in Fusion 360, eliminating bolted inter-arm joints so that structural loads flow through the part itself rather than through fasteners. Final frame mass: 385 g, FoS 8.93 under full thrust. Role: Design and analysis lead.

Contribution
  • 3-phase generative design in Fusion 360 — defined load cases (40 N thrust + 100 N·mm torsion per arm), iterated boundary conditions across phases; Phase III achieved target stiffness; committed to Nylon-12 SLS for final part
  • Full ANSYS structural suite: static (full thrust, asymmetric, landing), modal, transient, eigenvalue buckling (Mode 1 multiplier 8.65), explicit dynamics (15 m free-fall impact)
  • 3 CFD studies: 4-rotor propeller wake (validates 440 mm wheelbase), hover drag (0.272 N), 30° pitch drag (18.8 N)
  • Designed aluminium laser-cut landing gear for 100 N impact — FoS 3.35 under angled impact, buckling multiplier 8.65
  • Propulsion selection: T-Motor F90 2806.5 1300 KV + GF 7042 + 4200 mAh 6S Li-ion — 21.57 min hover endurance validated

Engineering Decisions

Generative Design — 3-Phase Iteration to SLS — Phase I arms showed excessive displacement under motor torque. Phase II added 100 N·mm torsional load per arm and lowered the thrust plane 20 mm — stiffness improved but arm cross-sections were too wide, raising drag. Phase III resolved both: narrower arms, correct thrust plane, target stiffness met. SLS was chosen over FDM for the final part because the resolved geometry — internal cavities, non-planar surfaces — is not printable by FDM without support scarring on structural faces.

440 mm Wheelbase, Set by CFD — 4-propeller transient CFD (K-ω SST, rotating domains) quantified the thrust loss between propellers as 5.64 N per propeller at 440 mm spacing — under 10% of a single propeller's thrust, which is an acceptable loss. Going smaller would have increased interference loss; going larger increased moment of inertia without aerodynamic benefit. Wheelbase was fixed before frame geometry was committed.

Li-ion over LiPo — Disaster-response missions involve sustained hover and moderate cruise (35–60% throttle) rather than short bursts of peak power. Li-ion energy density is higher in this regime. Battery was reduced from initial 4500 mAh to 4200 mAh after propulsion validation — endurance remained above target, frame mass dropped 40 g.

Validation
  • FEA Static: Max deformation 1.02 mm, max stress 8.93 MPa, FoS = 8.93 (full thrust). Asymmetric thrust: max deformation 2.41 mm — safe.
  • FEA Landing Gear: 100 N impact (5× weight), FoS = 3.35. Buckling multiplier Mode 1 = 8.65 — buckles only at 8.65× applied load.
  • Explicit Dynamics: 15 m free-fall onto steel plate at 17.38 m/s — landing gear absorbed full impact; main frame intact.
  • Modal: First 6 modes: 71.2, 89.9, 93.7, 95.8, 165.1, 165.4 Hz — all above motor RPM excitation range.
  • Full-Body CFD (K-ω SST, 4 rotating domains): Total thrust 61.76 N; hover drag 0.272 N; pitch drag (30°) 18.8 N.
Limitations & Next Steps
  • Phase II arm stiffness issue — including torsional load and drag constraint as a combined parametric objective from Phase I would reduce iteration time
  • Li-ion charges slower than LiPo; for multi-sortie operations an XT90 anti-spark hot-swap bay would reduce turnaround time

SAE Aero Design West 2025 · Micro Class · 11th / 75 International Teams
SAE Aero Design Micro Class Fixed-Wing UAV
Overview

High-wing semi-monocoque fixed-wing UAV for SAE Aero Design West 2025 (Van Nuys, CA). Carried a 67 oz liquid payload within a strict 450 W motor power cap. Validated aerodynamically via wind tunnel (12 pressure taps, Re = 2.41×10⁵) and ANSYS CFD — S1223 120% airfoil selection and teardrop fuselage geometry driven entirely by data. Placed 11th of 75 international teams. Role: Aerodynamics, spar sizing, stability, and wind tunnel lead.

Contribution
  • Multi-parameter airfoil trade study — selected S1223 at 120% thickness; validated Cl/Cd advantage over standard profile in wind tunnel at Re = 2.41×10⁵
  • Sized the main spar using bending moment analysis and the Flexure Formula under a 60 N gust load — selected a CFRP tube (12 mm outer diameter, 10 mm inner diameter) positioned at 17% of the wing chord for maximum structural efficiency
  • Topology-optimised balsa/aeroply ribs in Fusion 360 — 15% mass reduction per rib, spar and shear-web clearances preserved
  • Wind tunnel test: 12 × 3 mm copper pressure tubes, manometer-based Cp measurement at α = 0°; validated CFD Cp distribution
  • Stall testing with wool tufts attached to the upper wing surface — as airspeed drops, the tufts show where airflow separates; this confirmed the stall onset angle and the transition from smooth to turbulent flow
  • ANSYS Fluent CFD (Spalart-Allmaras): wing, fuselage, and full-body simulations — Cp distribution validated against wind tunnel data
  • ANSYS Static Structural + Modal FEA; confirmed no resonance overlap between motor excitation and natural frequencies at cruise throttle

Engineering Decisions

S1223 at 120% Thickness — Standard S1223 generates insufficient lift at 4 kg AUW in the low-speed, power-limited regime. 120% thickness raises the structural spar depth and increases Clmax without an unacceptable drag penalty — at 130–140%, Cl/Cd collapses from 56 to 32. Wind tunnel at Re = 2.41×10⁵ confirmed higher Cp distribution over the modified profile vs. the standard section.

Teardrop Fuselage — Drag vs. Volume Trade — CFD comparison at 16 m/s: teardrop 0.705 N drag, circular 1.368 N, square 1.156 N. Teardrop was selected despite being harder to fabricate because circular cross-section did not provide enough internal volume for the 67 oz payload tank alongside electronics. Formers were laser-cut and jigged for alignment control.

Aileron-as-Flap for Take-off — Symmetric aileron deflection increases effective camber and Cl by over 20%, shortening take-off roll without adding a separate flap mechanism. Calculated ground roll: 14.18 m vs. 30.48 m competition limit. The pilot retracts the ailerons to neutral before any roll manoeuvre — this is a procedural step, not a system limitation, and the pilot was trained to do this before competition.

CFRP Spar at 17% MAC — Positioning the spar at 17% MAC allows it to also function as leading-edge reinforcement, eliminating a separate LE member. Sized for 60 N gust load (1.5× full lift): OD 12 mm, ID 10 mm CFRP tube. Static load test to 60 N equivalent — no permanent deformation.

Validation
  • CFD Wing (Spalart-Allmaras): With endplates — Lift 64.1 N, Drag 4.94 N, L/D 12.97. Without endplates: L/D 11.99. Endplates adopted.
  • CFD Full-Body: Lift 49.4 N, Drag 7.24 N at 16 m/s.
  • FEA Static: 120 N gust load; max deformation 26 mm; topology optimisation reduced rib mass 20%.
  • FEA Modal: 10 natural frequencies (12.6–159.6 Hz) — no overlap with motor excitation at 70–80% throttle.
  • Wind Tunnel: Cp vs. chord at α = 0°, Re = 2.41×10⁵ — experimental data matched CFD prediction within acceptable scatter.
  • Stall Test: Wool tufts on upper surface — confirmed flow separation onset angle consistent with S1223 prediction.
  • Thrust Bench: Static thrust matched Gabriel Staples dynamic thrust equation; confirmed ≥27 N within 450 W.
Limitations & Next Steps
  • Aileron-as-flap requires pilot discipline and a dedicated failsafe switch — a Fowler flap would give independent authority and cleaner aerodynamic analysis
  • 67 oz liquid payload shifts CG dynamically — baffled tank or foam insert would reduce in-flight CG excursion
  • Endurance tight at 2–3 min; 2200 mAh 4S within same weight envelope extends endurance ~22% at <60 g penalty

SAE Drone Development Challenge — Regular Class Fixed-Wing UAV
Overview

High-wing fixed-wing UAV developed across two competition cycles for the SAEISS Drone Development Challenge. Objective: maximise payload fraction while executing a 500 g cargo airdrop. The two-season iteration — same aerodynamic configuration, refined structure — improved placement from 4th to 1st. Research from the design evolution was presented at FMFP 2025. Role: Junior Member (DDC 2024) then Team Captain (DDC 2025) — system design, structural analysis, payload mechanism, and report.

Contribution
  • Full design cycle: airfoil selection (S1223 120%), wing sizing, XFLR5 stability, CFD-driven fuselage shaping, 4-motor propulsion trade study
  • Wind tunnel: 12 copper pressure taps on S1223 120% wing at Re = 4.08×10⁵ — confirmed Cp advantage of 120% thickness over standard profile

  • Payload prediction model coded in C: a formula that estimates how much payload the aircraft can carry based on the air density at the competition venue (Chennai conditions) — used on competition day to set a realistic target before the flight
  • MG995 servo gate-release payload drop mechanism — CG shift post-drop verified within stable envelope
  • Authored the design report as junior member (DDC 2024) — team awarded 2nd Best Design Report; work continued as captain in DDC 2025 cycle, extended into FMFP 2025 paper

Engineering Decisions

S1223 120% + Trailing-Edge Taper — Highest Clmax (2.30) and stall angle (14°) in the trade study. Trailing-edge taper improves spanwise lift distribution and reduces induced drag vs. a rectangular planform — manufacturing complexity is manageable with laser-cut rib templates. Full-body CFD at 18 m/s gives 105 N lift against a 98.1 N MTOW requirement — 7% margin.

Cockpit-Type Fuselage over Circular — Circular cross-section has lower drag (0.682 N vs. 2.860 N at 18 m/s), but does not provide enough internal volume for a 10 kg aircraft's payload bay, battery, and electronics. The 2.18 N drag difference is less than 0.03% of available thrust — not a significant trade. The cockpit section also distributes landing loads better across a flat structural base. FoS 1.3 under combined 300 N vertical + 112 N horizontal landing load.

T-Motor AM-670 520 KV — Selected from a 4-motor comparison on thrust-to-mass ratio (8,576 g thrust at 416 g motor mass) and lowest winding resistance (0.010 Ω) — both indicate efficient sustained-torque operation at high throttle. Bench test confirmed analytical thrust values.

Nose Gear Spring Suspension — Landing load spikes to ~300 N on a 10 kg aircraft at field surface conditions. A spring absorber (k = 12,000 N/m, 25 mm travel) distributes the impulse over a longer contact duration, reducing the peak structural load at the fuselage attach point. FoS 1.06 — acceptable but tight; a 2 mm increase in strut OD would bring this above 1.3 at negligible weight cost.

Validation
  • CFD Wing (Spalart-Allmaras): With endplates — Lift 64.1 N, Drag 4.94 N, L/D 12.97. Without endplates: L/D 11.99. Endplates adopted.
  • CFD Full-Body: Lift 105.1 N, Drag 14.3 N at 18 m/s — 7% lift margin over 10 kg MTOW.
  • FEA Fuselage: 300 N vertical + 112 N horizontal landing load. Max deformation 0.8 mm, FoS 1.3.
  • FEA Landing Gear: Nose: deformation 3.2 mm, FoS 1.06. Main: deformation 0.499 mm, strain 0.00167 (Al yield 0.00285), FoS 2.27.
  • Wind Tunnel: S1223 120% shows higher Cp than standard S1223 at Re = 4.08×10⁵ — experimentally confirmed.
  • Modal: 10 modes (12.6–159.6 Hz) — no overlap with motor excitation at cruise throttle.
  • Payload Prediction Curve: Validated against test-day density altitude at Chennai (To = 303.15 K, Po = 100,500 Pa).
Limitations & Next Steps
  • Nose gear FoS = 1.06 — CFRP tube OD 14 mm (from 12 mm) would raise FoS to ~1.3 at ~15 g weight penalty
  • Post-drop CG shift validated analytically but not in isolated trim flight test — should be standard procedure in future cycles
  • The improvement from 4th to 1st between DDC 2024 and 2025 came from structural weight reduction and a more disciplined competition strategy, not from redesigning the aircraft — showing that refining an existing design can outperform starting over when time and budget are limited

Engineering Decisions

Tricycle over Taildragger — At 10 kg with nose-heavy electronics, a tricycle layout prevents the aircraft tipping forward during braking and protects the propeller on hard landings. The small weight penalty is worth it for the structural protection and easier ground handling it gives the pilot.

Spring Absorber on Nose Gear — 300 N spike over 25 mm travel distributes impulse over longer contact duration, reducing peak load transmitted to avionics bay. Housed within a CFRP tube of 20 mm outer diameter — no extra space needed outside the existing structure. The Wahl-corrected shear stress remains within the allowable limit for spring steel.

125° A-Frame on Main Gear — Wider effective track without increasing lateral span, improving ground roll stability. 4 mm Al 6061-T6 is the minimum thickness achieving FoS > 2.0 under combined load; topology optimisation removed further material where stress was low, yielding ~20% mass reduction.

  • FEA Main Gear (ANSYS): Same load. Max deformation 0.499 mm, strain 0.00167 (well within Al yield 0.00285), FoS = 2.27.
  • Topology optimisation (Fusion 360): Representative loads applied; material removed where stress was consistently low — ~20% mass reduction while keeping the load path between gear and fuselage intact.
  • Spring Validation: k = F/δ = 300/0.025 = 12,000 N/m confirmed analytically; Wahl correction factor applied.
Limitations & Next Steps
  • Nose gear FoS = 1.06 — CFRP strut OD 14 mm (from 12 mm) raises FoS to ~1.3 at ~15 g weight penalty
  • Crosswind side loads not modelled — lateral force case (~15–20% of vertical) should be added in future design revision
  • Servo-steered nose wheel (MG90S) as implemented in the DDC 2026 VTOL configuration would improve ground handling for precision cargo-drop positioning

Key Design Decisions

Stage Ratio Split: 2:1 then 3:1

Placing the lower ratio at Stage 1 means Stage 1 gears handle higher speed at lower torque — allowing a smaller module (mn = 3 mm) and lighter gears. Stage 2 carries the full output torque and was given mn = 4 mm to handle the higher bending and contact loads. This split minimises total gear mass while meeting strength requirements.

Material Pairing: 20MnCr5 Pinion / C45 Gear

Case-hardened pinion (650 HB) against normalised gear (200 HB) — standard industrial pairing. Harder pinion resists the higher cycle count it sees (rotates faster than gear). C45 gear provides adequate wear resistance at lower cost. Contact stress limit governs Stage 1 wear FOS (1.28 vs. minimum 1.2) — chosen as the governing check for module selection.

Shaft Design by DE-Goodman Fatigue Criterion

Shafts are subjected to combined bending and torsion with stress concentrations from keyways (Kf = 1.54, Kt = 1.6, q = 0.9). The corrected endurance limit Se accounts for surface, size, and reliability (99%) factors. Output shaft governs with the highest torque (286,598 N·mm) — fatigue FOS = 2.34, selected dia Ø25 mm.

Bearing Selection: DGBB vs. Angular Contact

Deep groove ball bearings (SKF 6303/6304) were selected over angular contact bearings because axial loads (Pa = 567–926 N) remain within DGBB axial capacity, avoiding the additional assembly complexity and cost of angular contact pairs. L₁₀ life exceeds 20,000 hr design target on all three shafts — output shaft achieves 50,928 hr due to lowest rotational speed.

Tricopter VTOL Test Platform
Overview

280 mm wheelbase rapid-prototype tricopter built to de-risk the servo-actuated rear-motor tilt yaw control mechanism before integrating it into the competition Hybrid VTOL Tricopter UAV. Fabricated using laser-cut interlocking tab-and-slot MDF joints — full airframe produced in under 48 hours. Validated hover stability and yaw authority at varying tilt angles before the mechanism was committed to CFRP production.

Contribution
  • Designed laser-cut MDF tab-and-slot frame — full interlocking geometry, no adhesive required for assembly or disassembly
  • Sized and integrated servo-actuated rear motor tilt mount; characterised tilt angle vs. yaw moment relationship in hover
  • Configured ArduPilot tricopter firmware; tuned initial PID gains for stable hover on test platform
  • Used platform to validate tilt servo travel limits and mechanical stop positions before finalising KST X10 Pro A selection for competition aircraft
Key Specifications
Wheelbase280 mm
Frame MaterialLaser-cut MDF — tab-and-slot interlocking joints
Rear Motor MechanismServo-actuated tilt for yaw control
Fabrication Time<48 hours (design to first hover)
PurposeTilt-mechanism validation before competition airframe commitment
Engineering Decisions

MDF Tab-and-Slot over 3D Print — Laser cutting produces flat parts in minutes vs. hours for FDM. Tab-and-slot joints provide precise, repeatable alignment without adhesive, allowing the mechanism mounting position to be adjusted between test flights. Trade-off: MDF is heavier than equivalent carbon structure — accepted for a ground/low-altitude validation platform.

Dedicated Test Platform over Direct Integration — Committing the tilt mechanism directly to the CFRP competition airframe would risk unrecoverable damage if the mechanism behaviour was unpredictable. A low-cost MDF test bed (under ₹800 in materials) meant that any early failures or design changes happened to cheap prototypes, not to the finished competition aircraft.

Outcome

Yaw authority confirmed across full servo travel range. Tilt angle limits and rate of travel validated against ArduPilot mixer requirements. PID baseline established and transferred to the competition aircraft configuration. The mechanism behaved as the analysis predicted, giving confidence to proceed with integrating it into the final carbon-fibre competition aircraft.

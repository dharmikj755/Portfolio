Project Detail
SAE Aero Design East 2026 · Advanced Class
Hybrid VTOL Tricopter UAV
Overview

This hybrid VTOL tricopter UAV is being developed for the SAE Aero Design East 2026 Advanced Class competition. The aircraft is designed to take off vertically, transition to fixed-wing cruise flight, and deliver a payload to a designated target area.

The design uses a lifting-body fuselage to generate additional lift during cruise, reducing the aerodynamic penalty of the fuselage and improving overall efficiency. A front-mounted tilting motor configuration provides vertical thrust, with the tilt mechanism validated on the Tricopter Test Platform.

Key Specifications
Wingspan~800 mm
AUW~1.6 kg
MotorsT-Motor F80 Pro
Flight ControllerTBS Lucid H7 Wing
StructureBalsa/Aeroply + Carbon Fibre
Design Approach

The lifting-body fuselage cross-section was aerodynamically shaped to generate lift at cruise angles of attack, reducing the weight penalty of a conventional box fuselage. FEA validation confirmed a 10% weight reduction from the front-mounted tilt configuration.

The transition sequence — vertical takeoff, forward tilt, fixed-wing cruise, and vertical landing — was modelled in ANSYS and validated through flight testing.

Aerothon 2025 · Disaster Response UAV
Aerothon Quadcopter
Overview

Designed and built for the Aerothon 2025 disaster-response UAV challenge. The quadcopter features an X-frame configuration with a generative-design single-piece PET-G frame, reducing part count while optimising load paths.

Propulsion was sized to achieve a thrust-to-weight ratio of ~4.58 using 1300 KV BLDC motors on a 440 mm wheelbase, ensuring stable hover and responsive handling in confined environments.

Key Specifications
Wheelbase440 mm
Motors1300 KV BLDC
TWR~4.58
Frame MaterialPET-G (Generative Design)
Design Approach

The airframe was designed using Fusion 360's generative design solver with structural constraints derived from expected flight loads, producing a lightweight single-piece frame that eliminated traditional multi-part assembly.

ANSYS structural analysis verified the frame under maximum thrust and landing impact scenarios before fabrication by 3D printing.

SAE Aero Design West 2025 · Micro Class · 11th / 75 Teams
Micro Class Fixed-Wing UAV
Overview

High-wing semi-monocoque fixed-wing UAV developed for SAE Aero Design West 2025, Van Nuys CA. The aircraft was designed to carry a 2 L liquid payload while operating within a strict 450 W motor power limit.

The design prioritised aerodynamic efficiency and structural weight savings to maximise payload fraction within the power constraint. Achieved 11th place among 75 international teams at the international competition.

Key Specifications
Wingspan1.312 m
Payload2 L liquid
Power Limit450 W
Result11th / 75 international teams
Design Approach

Wing geometry and aerofoil selection were driven by low-speed lift requirements and the 450 W power constraint. Aerodynamic performance was assessed using ANSYS Fluent for CFD analysis and XFLR5 for panel-method validation — results presented in a paper at FMFP 2025.

Semi-monocoque construction with a balsa/aeroply skin over a lightweight rib-and-spar structure balanced structural integrity with minimum weight at 1.312 m wingspan.

SAE DDC 2024 & 2025 · Regular Class · 1st Overall 2025
Regular Class Fixed-Wing UAV
Overview

Regular class fixed-wing UAV developed across two competition cycles (DDC 2024 and DDC 2025). Conventional tractor configuration with a box fuselage and high-wing layout optimised for payload capacity and structural efficiency.

The aircraft carries a 500 g airdrop payload and was refined over two seasons — achieving 4th Overall and 2nd Best Design Report in 2024, then 1st Overall among 100+ teams in 2025.

Key Specifications
Wingspan1.68 m
Empty Weight~3.6 kg
AUW~10 kg
MotorT-Motor AM670 520KV
Prop16×10
BatteryLiPo 6S 3300mAh 40C
StructureBalsa/Aeroply + Aluminium Spar
Design Approach

Aluminium main spar carries primary bending loads while balsa/aeroply skin panels handle shear. Topology optimisation of brackets and fittings reduced structural weight from 4.1 kg to 3.6 kg across the two competition cycles.

The conventional tail provides pitch and yaw authority for stable payload release. Iterative design reviews between 2024 and 2025 refined the structural layout, manufacturing process, and competition strategy leading to the championship result.

SAE DDC · Structural Component
Primary Landing Gear — 10 kg UAV
Overview

A lightweight tricycle landing gear assembly designed for the 10 kg gross-weight regular class UAV. The system uses carbon-fibre rods as the primary structural element, with CNC-machined aluminium clamps and an expanding spring mechanism to absorb landing loads.

The assembly achieves ~250 g total mass at 235 mm height, meeting the competition requirement for ground clearance during airdrop operations.

Key Specifications
ConfigurationTricycle
Assembly Weight~250 g
Height235 mm
Design Gross Weight10 kg
MaterialCarbon-fibre rods + CNC aluminium
Design Approach

Load cases were derived from the 10 kg gross weight aircraft landing with a safety factor applied for the competition drop-payload scenario. The carbon-fibre rod diameter and aluminium clamp geometry were sized to remain within allowable stress limits under these loads.

The expanding spring mechanism was selected to dissipate landing energy progressively, preventing hard contact transfer to the fuselage structure. All machined components were fabricated in-house using the team CNC setup.

Internal Test Platform · Mechanism Validation
Tricopter UAV Test Platform
Overview

Rapid-fabricated tricopter test platform built specifically to validate the servo-actuated rear motor tilt mechanism for yaw control before integration onto the full VTOL aircraft.

Laser-cut MDF interlocking tab-and-slot construction kept fabrication time minimal and allowed fast iteration on the tilt mechanism geometry and servo linkage geometry during flight testing.

Key Specifications
Wheelbase280 mm
FrameLaser-cut MDF
MotorsiFlight XING 2806.5 1300KV
Flight ControllerCrossflight FC
Yaw ControlServo-actuated rear motor tilt
Design Approach

The tricopter configuration was chosen because it directly replicates the yaw authority approach used on the VTOL — a rear motor that tilts via servo actuation rather than using a tail rotor. This isolated the mechanism validation from the complexity of the full VTOL airframe.

The MDF tab-and-slot frame was designed to be rapidly replaceable between test sessions. PID tuning was performed using Mission Planner on the Crossflight FC, with the tilt servo response characterised across the full range of yaw demand inputs.

About Me
Introduction

I’m Dharmik Umeshkumar Jha, a third-year Mechanical Engineering student at Nirma University, Ahmedabad, pursuing a minor in Data Science. I serve as Captain of SAE Team Arrow, leading a multidisciplinary team that designs, builds, and competes with UAVs at national and international competitions.

My work spans the full engineering cycle — from aerodynamic analysis and structural design through to fabrication and flight testing. I have designed and flight-tested more than five UAV platforms, including fixed-wing aircraft, hybrid VTOL tricopters, and multi-rotor systems, developing each from initial concept through CFD and FEA to competition day.

I hold my work to a standard that goes beyond the competition result. I presented a paper at FMFP 2025 and have a journal manuscript in preparation for the Journal of Flow Visualization and Image Processing. My goal is engineering that is analytically rigorous, physically validated, and documented well enough to be built on.

Engineering Philosophy

I’m drawn to problems where digital design meets physical reality — where a part modelled in Fusion 360 or SolidWorks has to survive a real load case, a manufacturing process, or a competition flight. That translation from drawing to working hardware is where I find the most interesting engineering.

My focus is on mechanical design and structural engineering — configuring airframes, sizing components, and optimising assemblies for minimum weight at maximum reliability. I have a strong interest in generative and topology-optimised design as a method for producing parts that are both lighter and better-resolved than conventional approaches. I see data science as a practical tool within this: analysing test results, supporting design decisions, and shortening the iteration loop.
